Every year on **4 December**, India celebrates **Navy Day** — marking the 1971 Operation Trident strike on Karachi harbour that turned the tide of the Bangladesh war. It is also a good moment to understand how the Indian Navy actually works: its command structure, the ships it operates, and where it is heading.

This guide uses only publicly available, open-source information.

---

## Why the Indian Navy Matters

India has a **7,516 km coastline**, two island chains (Andaman & Nicobar and Lakshadweep), and sits at the centre of the Indian Ocean Region (IOR) — one of the world's busiest sea-lanes. Around **80% of India's oil imports and 95% of its trade by volume** moves by sea.

The Indian Navy's job is to keep those sea-lanes open, protect the coastline, respond to disasters, and project power when needed.

---

## How the Navy Is Organised: Three Commands

The Indian Navy is divided into **three operational commands**, each headed by a **Flag Officer Commanding-in-Chief** (FOC-in-C) of Vice Admiral rank:

| Command | Headquarters | Area of Responsibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Western Naval Command** | Mumbai | Arabian Sea, Persian Gulf, Red Sea |
| **Eastern Naval Command** | Visakhapatnam (Vizag) | Bay of Bengal, Andaman Sea |
| **Southern Naval Command** | Kochi | Training, education, logistics base |

> [!NOTE]
> The Southern Command is the **training command** — it runs the Indian Naval Academy (INA) at Ezhimala, Kerala, which commissions officers into all three services.

The **Chief of Naval Staff (CNS)** — an Admiral — sits at the top, based in New Delhi, and reports to the **Chief of Defence Staff (CDS)** under the integrated theatre command structure India is currently building.

## The Ships: What Does India Operate?

The Navy publicly releases fleet information through official PIB (Press Information Bureau) releases and the annual Ministry of Defence report. Here is what is publicly known:

### Aircraft Carriers
India is one of only a few countries to operate **aircraft carriers**. It currently has two:

**INS Vikrant (IAC-1)**
- India's first **indigenously built** aircraft carrier, commissioned in September 2022
- Built at Cochin Shipyard Limited (CSL) — a major milestone for Indian shipbuilding
- Displacement: ~45,000 tonnes (fully loaded)
- Uses a **STOBAR** (Short Take-Off But Arrested Recovery) system — ski-jump ramp for launch, arrester wires for landing
- Operates MiG-29K fighters and Ka-31 helicopters (with more aircraft to follow)
- Length: ~262 metres

![INS Vikrant (IAC-1) sailing at sea](/images/navy-ins-vikrant.jpg)
*India's first indigenous aircraft carrier, INS Vikrant (IAC-1), underway in the Indian Ocean.*

**INS Vikramaditya (R33)**
- Former Russian carrier *Admiral Gorshkov*, refitted and commissioned into the Indian Navy in 2013
- Displacement: ~45,500 tonnes
- Also uses a STOBAR system
- Based at INS Hansa, Goa (Dabolim)
- Operates MiG-29K fighters

![INS Vikramaditya overhead view](/images/navy-ins-vikramaditya.jpg)
*Overhead view of INS Vikramaditya (R33) with MiG-29K multirole fighters and naval helicopters parked on the flight deck.*

> [!TIP]
> STOBAR (ski-jump + arrestor wires) is different from the American CATOBAR system (steam/electromagnetic catapult). The ski-jump method limits the aircraft's fuel and weapons load at launch. India is reportedly studying catapult systems for its next carrier (IAC-2).

### Destroyers and Frigates

These are the backbone of the fleet — large, well-armed surface ships that escort carriers and patrol sea-lanes.

**Destroyers (largest surface combatants):**
- **Kolkata class (Project 15A)** — 3 ships; Indian-designed; anti-ship, anti-air, anti-submarine capable
- **Visakhapatnam class (Project 15B)** — 4 ships ordered; first two commissioned (INS Visakhapatnam 2021, INS Mormugao 2022); among the most advanced destroyers India has built
- **Delhi class (Project 15)** — 3 older destroyers; still in service

![INS Visakhapatnam (D66) guided missile destroyer](/images/navy-destroyer-visakhapatnam.jpg)
*INS Visakhapatnam (D66), lead ship of the Indian Navy's Project 15B stealth guided-missile destroyers.*

**Frigates:**
- **Shivalik class (Project 17)** — 3 ships; stealth-shaped hulls; multi-role
- **Nilgiri class (Project 17A)** — 7 ships ordered; improved stealth; deliveries underway
- **Talwar class** — 6 Russian-built frigates (3 from Russia, 3 more added); armed with BrahMos missiles

---

### Corvettes and Patrol Vessels

Smaller ships used for coastal patrol, anti-submarine work, and island surveillance:

- **Kamorta class (Project 28)** — 4 ASW (Anti-Submarine Warfare) corvettes
- **Abhay class** — older corvettes
- **Sukanya class** — offshore patrol vessels
- Multiple **fast attack craft** and **missile boats** (Veer and Prabal class)

---

### Submarines: The Silent Force

Submarines are the most strategically sensitive part of the fleet. India publicly acknowledges the following:

**Conventional (Diesel-Electric) Submarines:**
- **Kalvari class (Project 75)** — Scorpène-design submarines built at Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders, Mumbai. 6 submarines ordered; all 6 now commissioned (INS Kalvari through INS Vagsheer). Quiet, modern, capable of firing torpedoes and anti-ship missiles.
- **Sindhughosh class** — Older Kilo-class submarines from Russia; most being phased out
- **Shishumar class** — HDW (German-designed) submarines; ageing but still in service

![Kalvari-class submarine surfaced at sea](/images/navy-kalvari-submarine.jpg)
*A Kalvari-class diesel-electric submarine (Project 75) sailing on the surface.*

**Nuclear-Powered Submarines (SSBNs):**
- **INS Arihant** — India's first indigenously built ballistic missile submarine (SSBN), commissioned 2016. Carries K-15 Sagarika submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs). Gives India a **sea-based nuclear deterrent** — the third leg of its nuclear triad.
- **INS Arighaat** — Second SSBN of the same class, commissioned 2024.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> India does **not** publicly confirm specific operational details of its nuclear submarines beyond official government statements. Everything above comes from official PIB releases and the MoD annual report.

### Naval Aviation: The Airborne Arm

The Indian Navy operates its own aircraft:

| Aircraft | Role |
|---|---|
| MiG-29K / MiG-29KUB | Carrier-based fighters |
| P-8I Poseidon | Maritime patrol and anti-submarine (from Boeing USA) |
| IL-38SD | Older maritime patrol aircraft (Russian) |
| Dornier 228 | Coastal surveillance |
| Ka-31 | Airborne early warning helicopter (carrier-based) |
| Ka-28 | Anti-submarine helicopter |
| Sea King Mk.42 | Anti-submarine and SAR helicopter (ageing) |
| MH-60R Seahawk | New multi-role naval helicopter (from USA) |

The P-8I is considered India's most capable maritime patrol aircraft — it can track submarines, surface ships, and conduct intelligence gathering over vast ocean areas.

## The Andaman & Nicobar Command: India's Strategic Island Chain

The **Andaman and Nicobar Command (ANC)** is India's only **tri-service command** (Army + Navy + Air Force together), based at Port Blair. It sits at the mouth of the Strait of Malacca — through which a large share of East Asian trade passes.

This gives India a strategic vantage point over the Indian Ocean that it is steadily developing with longer runways, better port facilities, and more regular naval patrols.

## What Is India Building Next?

Based on official government announcements and public budget documents:

- **IAC-2 (Third aircraft carrier):** Under consideration. The government has not yet formally sanctioned it. If built, it may use a catapult launch system (CATOBAR) for heavier aircraft loads.
- **Project 75I (6 more submarines):** A major procurement to follow Project 75. Requirements include Air-Independent Propulsion (AIP) for longer underwater endurance. Tender still in progress.
- **Project 17A frigates:** 7 Nilgiri-class frigates; construction ongoing at Mazagon Dock and Garden Reach Shipbuilders (GRSE).
- **Next-generation destroyers (Project 15C):** Early design stages.
- **MH-60R Seahawk helicopters:** 24 ordered from USA; deliveries ongoing — replacing the ageing Sea Kings.

India's broad direction is **Aatmanirbharta** (self-reliance): building as many ships as possible in Indian shipyards at Mazagon Dock (Mumbai), CSL (Kochi), and GRSE (Kolkata).

## Navy Day: What Happened on 4 December 1971?

**Operation Trident** was a missile boat attack on Karachi harbour during the 1971 India-Pakistan war. Three Indian missile boats — INS Nipat, INS Nirghat, and INS Veer — fired Styx anti-ship missiles and hit Pakistani fuel storage tanks and a minesweeper. Karachi harbour was lit on fire. It was the **first use of anti-ship missiles in combat in Asia**, and it crippled Pakistan's naval logistics. Navy Day marks this strike.

## How to Join the Indian Navy

All entry routes are through **official government channels only**. The main paths:

| Entry | For | How |
|---|---|---|
| NDA (National Defence Academy) | 10+2 students (men) | UPSC NDA exam |
| 10+2 B.Tech Cadet Entry | 10+2 PCM students | JEE score based |
| University Entry Scheme (UES) | Final-year engineering students | Direct from campus |
| Short Service Commission (SSC) | Graduates in various streams | Notification-based |
| Sailor Entry (MR / NMR / AA) | 10th / 10+2 pass | Navy recruitment rallies |
| Agniveer (Navy) | 17.5–21 years | Under Agnipath scheme |

Official notifications are published only on **joinindiannavy.gov.in**.
