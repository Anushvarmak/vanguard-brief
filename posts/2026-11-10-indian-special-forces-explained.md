Each of India's three armed services has its own special force, and since 2019 a joint division has linked them. Here is who they are, what each one does, and why they are in the news again.

"Commando" is one of the most used and most misused words in Indian defence talk. Para SF, MARCOS, Garud, NSG and even regular infantry teams all get called commandos, yet they are very different. In this post, I explain it step by step. Everything here comes from open sources, and I do not describe any operational detail.

## In 30 seconds

- India has three military special forces: **Para (SF)** in the Army, **MARCOS** in the Navy and the **Garud Commando Force** in the Air Force.
- Since 2019, a tri-service body called the **Armed Forces Special Operations Division (AFSOD)** has brought them together for joint missions and training.
- You cannot join any of them straight from civilian life. You first join the parent service, then **volunteer** and pass a very tough selection.
- In 2025, the Army announced **Bhairav light commando battalions**, a new layer between regular infantry and Para SF.


*India's special forces at a glance: three service forces, one joint division, and two related forces shown separately*

## What does "special forces" mean?

Special forces are small teams that are selected, trained and equipped to do tasks that ordinary units cannot. A regular infantry battalion has about 800 soldiers and is built to hold ground. A special forces team is a handful of people built to do something precise and difficult. Typical tasks are:

- **Special reconnaissance:** quietly watching and reporting on an enemy or a target.
- **Direct action:** short, planned raids against a specific target.
- **Counter-terrorism:** dealing with terrorist threats, including hostage rescue.
- **Unconventional warfare:** working in difficult conditions or behind enemy lines, sometimes with local forces.

They are "special" because of **selection and training**, not because of weapons. Everyone in these forces is a volunteer, and most volunteers do not make it through.

## The three military special forces

| Force | Service | Raised | Main role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Para (SF) | Indian Army | 1966 | Land operations: special reconnaissance, direct action, counter-terrorism |
| MARCOS | Indian Navy | 1987 | Maritime and amphibious operations, counter-terrorism at sea |
| Garud Commando Force | Indian Air Force | 2004 | Protecting and seizing airfields, special reconnaissance, combat search and rescue |
| AFSOD | Joint (all three) | 2018 (operational 2019) | Joint special operations |

### Para (SF): the Army's special forces

![Para (SF) Balidaan badge](/images/para-sf-insignia.png)

*The Para (SF) Balidaan badge.*

Para (SF) battalions are the special forces side of the **Parachute Regiment**. They were raised in 1966, after the 1965 war, from earlier parachute commando units. Reports say one Parachute Regiment battalion was converted to a special forces role in 1978, and more were converted over the following decades.

The Parachute Regiment's motto is "Shatrujeet", which means "Conqueror of Enemies". The Army does not publish how many Para (SF) battalions it has. Open sources have given figures from about nine to about fifteen, depending on the year, so treat any exact number with care.

One point causes much confusion. **"Para" and "Para (SF)" are not the same.** Many Parachute Regiment battalions are airborne infantry, which means they are trained to be dropped by parachute. Only the designated SF battalions are special forces.

![Para (SF) personnel in a forest](/images/parasf-group.png)

*Para (SF) personnel.*

Para SF is best known to the public for the cross-border operation across the Line of Control in September 2016, which the Indian government publicly acknowledged. Reports linked Para SF to it. I do not discuss details of any operation.

### MARCOS: the Navy's marine commandos

![MARCOS emblem](/images/marcos-insignia.png)

*MARCOS emblem.*

MARCOS stands for **Marine Commandos**. The idea began in 1985 and the force was raised in February 1987. It was first called the Indian Marine Special Force, then renamed the Marine Commando Force. The short name MARCOS came afterwards.

They are specialists in operations from the sea, rivers and creeks, in amphibious operations, and in maritime counter-terrorism. Their strength is classified. Published estimates range from about 1,200 to 2,000, so treat those as rough guesses. All MARCOS are volunteers from within the Navy. Reports say the Navy was helped in the early years by foreign special forces, and that MARCOS still train alongside Para SF for a short period.

MARCOS were part of the response to the 2008 Mumbai attacks.

![MARCOS personnel with a helicopter](/images/marcos-group.png)

### Garud Commando Force: the Air Force's special forces

![Garud Commando Force insignia](/images/garud-insignia.png)

*Garud Commando Force insignia.*

The Garud Commando Force was formed in **September 2004**. The reason was practical. After attacks on air bases in Jammu and Kashmir in 2001, the Air Force decided it needed its own trained force to protect its critical bases and aircraft instead of depending on others.

The name comes from Garuda, the divine bird of Hindu tradition. Garud tasks include protecting and seizing airfields, special reconnaissance, air assault and combat search and rescue (finding and recovering downed aircrew). Estimates of their strength range from about 1,000 to 1,500. Garud personnel are enlisted as airmen, which gives the Air Force its own route into the force.

## How do they work together? AFSOD

Before 2019, each force trained and operated separately. The **Armed Forces Special Operations Division (AFSOD)** was created in 2018 and became operational in 2019. It sits under the Integrated Defence Staff and brings together elements of Para SF, MARCOS and Garud so they can plan, train and operate as one team. Reports at its creation described a planned strength of about 3,000 personnel, headed by a Major General. Treat that figure as an estimate.

You can think of AFSOD as India's step towards a single Special Operations Command, as the United States has. It is not that yet, but the direction of travel is clear.

| Ghatak platoons | Army infantry battalions | Specially trained commando platoons inside regular battalions |

## The newest layer: Bhairav light commando battalions

On **26 July 2025**, at Kargil Vijay Diwas, the Army Chief announced the **Bhairav Light Commando Battalions**. They are designed to sit **between regular infantry and Para SF**.

- Each battalion has about **250 soldiers**, far fewer than a regular infantry battalion.
- The soldiers come from existing infantry and support units rather than from fresh recruitment.
- They are meant for fast, small-team missions in border areas and use modern equipment, including drones.
- Numbers differ by source. A July 2026 think-tank brief says about 15 are operational out of 25 planned, and another report mentions a 16th being raised.

**Bhairav battalions are not special forces.** Analysts describe them as enablers for regular infantry that free up the elite SF units for the most sensitive missions. They were showcased at the January 2026 Army Day parade.

## How are units named?

Para (SF) battalions use the number of the battalion followed by the regiment, for example "9 PARA (SF)". Those who pass the special forces probation are reportedly given the "Balidaan" badge, which is worn on the uniform. MARCOS and Garud are single forces rather than regiments, so they do not use battalion numbers in the same way.

## Three decorated special forces personnel

India's gallantry awards honour those who served in these forces. The **Ashoka Chakra** is India's highest peacetime gallantry award. Three recipients are:

- **Major Mohit Sharma, 1 Para (SF).** Awarded the Ashoka Chakra (posthumous) for a counter-terrorism operation in the Kupwara sector of Jammu and Kashmir on 21 March 2009.
- **Major Sandeep Unnikrishnan, 51 Special Action Group, NSG.** Awarded the Ashoka Chakra (posthumous) for his actions in the 2008 Mumbai attacks.
- **Havildar Gajender Singh Bisht, 51 Special Action Group, NSG.** Awarded the Ashoka Chakra (posthumous) for the 2008 Mumbai attacks.

> **Did you know?** Two of these three belonged to the NSG, not the military special forces. The NSG is under the Home Ministry, but its Special Action Group is drawn largely from the Army. That is why the NSG is so often confused with Para SF.

## Why are special forces in the news?

Two developments matter, and both are still unfolding.

- **A public joint doctrine.** On 27 August 2025, India released its first publicly available **Joint Doctrine for Special Forces Operations**, at the Ran Samwad seminar at the Army War College, Mhow. It sets out how the Army, Navy and Air Force special forces should plan, train and operate together. Reports say it also asks special forces to prepare for the information domain, not only the physical one, and points to a possible expansion of AFSOD and joint training institutes.
- **The Bhairav battalions.** The Army is still raising these units, and the final number is not settled.

## How do you join?

There is **no direct civilian entry** into any of these forces. You first join the parent service, then volunteer.

- **Para (SF):** join the Army as an officer or a soldier, serve in a unit, then volunteer for the special forces probation. Officers usually enter through the NDA, the CDS exam, or other officer routes. Soldiers enter through Army recruitment. Rules have changed since the Agnipath scheme, so please read the latest official notices.
- **MARCOS:** join the Navy, then volunteer for MARCOS selection. Only serving Navy personnel are eligible.
- **Garud:** join the Air Force as an airman and be selected for the Garud Commando Force. Check the Air Force's official recruitment notice for the current route.

Selection is famous for being tough, and the dropout rate is high. The exact tests and pass rates are not officially published, so be careful with websites that claim to know everything. What matters most is steady fitness, mental strength and discipline, which you can start building now.

## Common myths

- **"I can apply to Para SF after school."** No. You must first serve in the Army.
- **"Para commando means Para SF."** Not always. Many Para battalions are airborne infantry.
- **"NSG commandos are part of the Army."** The NSG is under the Home Ministry.
- **"Bhairav battalions are special forces."** They are light commando infantry, a layer below special forces.
- **"Movies show the real thing."** Real selection and operations are far quieter and more disciplined.

## Quick glossary

- **Special forces:** small, highly selected teams for difficult missions.
- **Para (SF):** the special forces battalions of the Parachute Regiment.
- **MARCOS:** the Navy's Marine Commando Force.
- **Garud:** the Air Force's special forces.
- **AFSOD:** Armed Forces Special Operations Division, the joint division.
- **Probation:** the tough selection and training period volunteers must pass.
- **Direct action:** a short, planned raid on a specific target.
- **Joint doctrine:** a written plan for how the three services work together.
- **Ashoka Chakra:** India's highest peacetime gallantry award.
