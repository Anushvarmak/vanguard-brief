Why can an F-35 fly directly through modern radar screens while a 4th-generation Su-30MKI or F-15 lights them up like a billboard? Why does China's J-20 use canards if canards supposedly ruin stealth? And where does India's Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft (AMCA) fit into the global race for fifth-generation air dominance?

In modern aviation, "Fifth Generation" is one of the most frequently used terms—and also one of the most misunderstood. It is not just about flying fast or carrying missiles; it is a fundamental shift in how combat aircraft are engineered, hidden from radar, and networked across battlefields.

In this explainer, we compare the four most discussed fifth-generation platforms—the American **F-35 Lightning II**, the Russian **Su-57 Felon**, the Chinese **J-20 Mighty Dragon**, and India's upcoming **AMCA**—using open-source engineering data and official releases.

## In 30 seconds

- **What defines a 5th-Gen fighter:** The combination of very low observability (stealth), active electronically scanned array (AESA) radar, sensor fusion, and internal weapon bays.
- **The F-35 (US & Allies):** The world's most mass-produced 5th-gen jet (1,000+ delivered). It prioritizes sensor fusion and battlefield networking over raw dogfighting speed.
- **The Su-57 (Russia):** Focused on super-maneuverability with 3D thrust vectoring and high speed, but trades off higher frontal and side radar cross-sections compared to Western stealth designs.
- **The J-20 (China):** A heavy, long-range interceptor built with a canard-delta configuration and large internal weapon bays, designed specifically to operate over the vast distances of the Indo-Pacific.
- **India's AMCA:** Cleared by the Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) in March 2024 with a ₹15,000 crore sanction. It features an indigenous S-duct air intake, Diverterless Supersonic Inlet (DSI), and Uttam AESA radar, with a two-phase engine roadmap (Mk1 with GE F414, Mk2 with an indigenous 110 kN power plant).

## What Actually Makes a Fighter "Fifth Generation"?

Generations in combat aviation reflect structural technology leaps:
- **3rd Generation (1960s–1970s):** Mach 2 speed, basic radar, early air-to-air missiles (e.g., MiG-21, F-4 Phantom).
- **4th & 4.5 Generation (1980s–Present):** Fly-by-wire controls, multi-role missions, glass cockpits, AESA radars (e.g., Su-30MKI, Rafale, F-16, Eurofighter Typhoon).
- **5th Generation:** Designed from the very first CAD drawing around **stealth** and **information superiority**.

To qualify as a true fifth-generation aircraft, an airframe must combine four foundational technological pillars:

1. **Very Low Observability (VLO / Stealth):**
   External weapons and fuel tanks reflect radar waves directly back to enemy receivers. Fifth-generation fighters store all missiles, bombs, and fuel inside **internal weapon bays**. Their fuselage surfaces are angled with aligned edges to deflect radar energy away from the threat radar.
2. **Sensor Fusion:**
   In older jets, the pilot had to look at separate screens for radar, infrared search-and-track (IRST), radar warning receivers, and communications. In a 5th-gen aircraft, a central mission computer merges data from all sensors into a single, seamless 360-degree tactical picture displayed on the pilot's helmet or panoramic screen.
3. **Advanced Avionics & Network-Centric Warfare:**
   The jet operates as a node in an airborne internet. An F-35 or AMCA can spot an enemy target silently with passive sensors and transmit targeting coordinates to a naval destroyer, ground SAM battery, or 4th-gen fighter without ever turning on its own active radar.
4. **Supercruise & Internal Carriage:**
   The ability to cruise at supersonic speeds without using fuel-guzzling afterburners. While the F-35 cannot easily supercruise, the F-22, Su-57, and J-20 incorporate supercruise engines, and AMCA Mk2 aims for it with its planned 110 kN engines.

## The Four Fighters Examined

### 1. Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II (United States & Global Fleet)

The F-35 is currently the only fifth-generation fighter produced at scale, with over 1,000 operational aircraft across more than 17 allied nations.

- **Design Philosophy:** The F-35 was not designed to out-turn an airshow fighter in a dogfight. Instead, it was built so that the pilot spots, identifies, and destroys the enemy 80 kilometers away before the opposing pilot even knows an aircraft is in the airspace.
- **Engine:** Powered by the Pratt & Whitney F135, the most powerful single combat turbofan engine in operational service, producing over 191 kN (43,000 lbf) of afterburning thrust.
- **Stealth Architecture:** Exceptional all-aspect stealth. Frontal Radar Cross Section (RCS) is estimated at roughly **0.0015 m²** (equivalent to a metal golf ball).
- **The Sensor Supercomputer:** Features the APG-81 AESA radar, the Electro-Optical Targeting System (EOTS), and the AN/AAQ-37 Distributed Aperture System (DAS), which stitches together imagery from six infrared cameras surrounding the fuselage directly onto the pilot's $400,000 helmet display.

### 2. Sukhoi Su-57 Felon (Russia)

Russia's response to Western stealth programs, the Su-57 first flew in 2010 and entered limited service with the Russian Aerospace Forces in 2020.

- **Design Philosophy:** Russian aeronautical doctrine emphasizes extreme maneuverability and kinetic survival over pure stealth. If an engagement degenerates into a close-range dogfight or visual arena, the Su-57 is built to dominate.
- **Engine:** Currently flying with AL-41F1 engines, transitioning to the newly developed *Izdeliye 30* (AL-51F1) engines featuring full **3D thrust vectoring nozzles** capable of vectoring pitch and yaw simultaneously.
- **Stealth Trade-offs:** The Su-57 uses blended wing-body lifting surfaces and internal bays, but lacks serpentine S-ducts to completely shield the front turbine fan blades from incoming radar waves. Its estimated RCS of **0.1 to 0.5 m²** makes it far stealthier than an Su-35, but noticeably less stealthy than an F-35 or F-22.
- **Infrared & Multi-band Radar:** Incorporates cheek-mounted and side-looking radar arrays alongside a 101KS *Atoll* optical-electronic suite, designed to track stealth aircraft by their heat signatures.

---

### 3. Chengdu J-20 Mighty Dragon (China)

Entering service with the People's Liberation Army Air Force (PLAAF) in 2017, the J-20 is Asia's first operational fifth-generation stealth aircraft.

- **Design Philosophy:** Built as a heavy, long-range air-superiority and strike fighter. With large internal fuel volume, it is optimized to operate deep over the Pacific without requiring vulnerable aerial refueling tankers.
- **Aerodynamic Layout:** Unique among 5th-gen jets for using a **canard-delta configuration** with canted twin vertical stabilizers and Diverterless Supersonic Inlets (DSI). While purists argue that forward canards increase radar reflections during maneuvering, Chinese engineers aligned the canard edges with the main delta wings to preserve frontal stealth in straight flight.
- **Engine Evolution:** Early production airframes flew on Russian AL-31FN engines, then transitioned to indigenous Shenyang WS-10C engines with serrated stealth exhaust nozzles. Newer batches are testing with high-thrust WS-15 engines to achieve full supercruise.
- **Weaponry:** Internal main bay accommodates four to six long-range PL-15 active-radar air-to-air missiles, complemented by side bays carrying PL-10 short-range dogfight missiles that can be pushed outside into the slipstream prior to firing.

---

### 4. DRDO / ADA AMCA (India's Advanced Medium Combat Aircraft)

India's AMCA is a twin-engine, multi-role fifth-generation fighter currently under development by the Aeronautical Development Agency (ADA) in partnership with the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL).

- **CCS Sanction:** On 7 March 2024, the Cabinet Committee on Security (CCS) officially cleared the AMCA program with a budget sanction of ₹15,000 crore ($1.8 billion), funding the fabrication of five operational flight prototypes.
- **Stealth Engineering:**
  - **Serpentine S-Duct Intake:** The air intake curves in an S-shape to completely hide the metallic turbine blades of the engine from incoming radar beams.
  - **Diverterless Supersonic Inlet (DSI):** Uses a carefully sculpted 3D compression bump instead of heavy mechanical splitter plates to slow down supersonic air, saving airframe weight and eliminating radar-reflecting cavities.
  - **Internal Weapon Bay:** Can carry four Beyond Visual Range Air-to-Air Missiles (such as the indigenous Astra Mk1, Mk2, and Mk3) inside its internal belly bay. For high-threat missions, weapons remain entirely internal; for low-threat missions, external pylons can be mounted in "non-stealth bomb truck" mode.
- **Phased Production Roadmap:**
  - **AMCA Mk1 (Prototype to Initial Production):** Powered by two proven American General Electric F414-GE-INS6 turbofans (delivering ~98 kN afterburning thrust each).
  - **AMCA Mk2 (Full Production):** Planned to be powered by a next-generation 110 kN indigenous engine developed through international co-development partnerships (such as Safran or Rolls-Royce), enabling sustained supercruise.
- **Avionics & Sensors:** Features the indigenous **Uttam AESA radar**, integrated electronic warfare (EW) suite, internal infrared search-and-track (IRST), and vehicle health monitoring.

## How Stealth Works: The Physics of RCS

A common question is: *Why can't any plane just be painted with radar-absorbing paint and become stealthy?*

Radar-Absorbent Material (RAM) coatings only account for a small fraction of stealth. **90% of radar stealth comes from airframe geometry**:

1. **Edge Alignment:**
   Notice how the leading edge of an F-35 or AMCA wing is parallel to the trailing edge of the opposite wing. This ensures that radar reflections are gathered and bounced into a tiny, specific spike in one safe direction, rather than scattering in all directions where detectors could see them.
2. **Hidden Turbine Blades:**
   A jet engine's spinning titanium compressor blades act as an enormous rotating radar reflector. Fifth-generation aircraft route air through curved **S-ducts** so that incoming radar beams hit radar-absorbent interior walls and cannot physically see the engine face.
3. **Internal Antennae & Sensors:**
   Antennas and pitot tubes protruding from an aircraft body act as antennas for enemy radar. 5th-gen aircraft embed their antennas directly into the skin of the composite wings and stabilizers.

## Test Yourself

Click on any question to view the verified answer.

Q: Why do fifth-generation fighters carry their weapons inside internal bays instead of on wings?
A: Weapons mounted on external wing pylons have sharp edges, fins, and metal brackets that reflect radar waves directly back to enemy radar stations. Carrying weapons internally keeps the outer fuselage completely smooth, maintaining the aircraft's stealth profile.

Q: Does the F-35 have supercruise capability?
A: The standard F-35 is not designed for sustained supercruise without afterburner. While it can maintain Mach 1.2 for a short duration under specific conditions, true long-range supercruise is featured on twin-engine interceptors like the F-22 Raptor and Su-57.

Q: What is a Diverterless Supersonic Inlet (DSI) and which jets use it?
A: A DSI replaces heavy mechanical boundary-layer diverter plates with a specially sculpted 3D aerodynamic "bump" in front of the intake. It compresses incoming supersonic air and redirects turbulent boundary air away, saving weight and eliminating radar-reflective cavities. Both the F-35, J-20, and India's AMCA use DSI technology.

Q: What are the two planned phases for India's AMCA engine?
A: AMCA Mk1 will be powered by two imported General Electric F414 engines (generating ~98 kN each). AMCA Mk2 is planned to feature a newly developed indigenous 110 kN engine built with an international co-development partner to provide full supercruise and domestic power-train independence.
